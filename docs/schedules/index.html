Schedules & grace periods

Check states

StateMeaning
WaitingCreated, and no ping received yet. Nightjar won’t alert until the first ping arrives.
UpThe last signal was a success and the next ping isn’t overdue.
DownA ping is overdue past its grace period, or the job reported a failure.
PausedMonitoring is off. Pings are recorded but don’t trigger alerts.

Period schedules

The simplest option: “this job runs every N minutes/hours/days.” After each ping, the next one is expected one period later. Use this when the exact clock time doesn’t matter, for example a queue drainer started by a process manager.

Cron schedules

Use the same expression as your crontab: five fields, minute hour day-of-month month day-of-week.

ExpressionRuns
*/15 * * * *every 15 minutes
0 2 * * *daily at 02:00
30 6 * * 1-5weekdays at 06:30
0 0 1 * *at midnight on the 1st of each month

After each ping, the next one is expected at the next time the expression matches, evaluated in the check’s time zone. Pick the same zone the scheduler uses. Most servers run cron in UTC, while GitHub Actions schedules are always UTC.

Grace periods

The grace period is how late a ping may arrive before it counts as missed. A backup that usually takes 6 minutes but sometimes takes 25 needs a grace period of at least 30 minutes if you ping on completion.

If you ping /start at the beginning and success at the end, you can keep the grace period short and use a maximum duration to catch runs that drag.

When alerts fire

Nightjar evaluates every check once a minute. A check goes down, and alerts are sent, when:

  1. it is up, and the expected time plus grace has passed with no ping, or
  2. it receives a /fail or non-zero exit-code ping.

You get one alert when a check goes down and one when it recovers, not one per minute.
